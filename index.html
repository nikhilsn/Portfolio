Case Studies

Two decisions, with full context and honest retrospectives.

Blynkk · 2025–26

Taking Blynkk from 0 to ~80 Daily Bookings

0→1 launch of a home-services marketplace in two months.

Context

SenseForgeAI embedded me as PM on the Blynkk account — a new home-services marketplace with no users, no supply, and no traction. The client needed a working product and real bookings fast.

Problem

No single north star existed. The temptation was to build everything at once. The real risk was shipping a feature-heavy product with no demand signal before the engagement window closed.

My Decision(s)

Defined a tight product vision, cut scope to the core booking loop, and prioritized features by direct user and ops feedback. Iterated in rapid cycles rather than committing to a long roadmap upfront.

What Shipped

A functional home-services marketplace with a complete booking flow. Later added a demand/supply analytics and forecasting system with supply-prediction models that reduced idle payout cases from 50%+ to ~10–20%.

Outcome

~80 daily bookings within 2 months. The client converted the engagement to a full-time in-house role (Feb 2026).

What I'd Do Differently

The principle that drove the right outcome was ruthlessly prioritising core UX over feature breadth — booking flow, payment, customer support, and professional assignment — rather than spreading effort across nice-to-have features. If I were starting again I'd lock that principle in writing on day one and use it as the explicit filter for every scope conversation, instead of letting it emerge through iteration. That would have saved a few early debates and let us move even faster.

Shyra · AI Product

Designing Shyra's Three-State Response Framework

Fixing generic AI advice for distressed users through structured conversation design.

Context

Shyra is an AI spiritual companion built on RAG over 500+ hours of transcribed content. Users arrive in vulnerable emotional states expecting grounded, source-backed guidance — not generic chatbot replies.

Problem

Conversation analysis revealed distressed users were receiving one-size-fits-all responses. The product was technically functional but emotionally tone-deaf at the moments that mattered most.

My Decision(s)

Designed a three-state response framework: emotional acknowledgment → guidance → direct answer. Constrained all answers to source material to prevent hallucination. Added multilingual support (English, Hindi, Hinglish) so users could engage in their native register.

What Shipped

Redesigned conversational flow with the new response framework, source-constrained RAG responses, and Hindi/Hinglish support built into the prompt and retrieval layer.

Outcome

After beta testing within a closed circle, users shared that they liked coming to the platform to get advice or simply to keep their heart light — the product was creating a space people genuinely wanted to return to.

What I'd Do Differently

The deeper insight that emerged was that Shyra works best when it first understands the user's intention, then listens actively, and only suggests if needed. Sometimes people just want to speak — so the system now waits and gently motivates users to share rather than rushing to answer. This also surfaced richer context, making responses far more relevant to where the user actually was emotionally. I'd invest in that intention-detection layer much earlier rather than treating it as a later refinement; it turned out to be the core of the product, not an add-on.
